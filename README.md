# energy_econometrics_vestas_oil

The **Vestas vs. Oil Econometrics Project** is an empirical quantitative study designed to test the financial "substitution effect" between traditional fossil fuels and renewable energy assets. It investigates whether crude oil price fluctuations directly drive equity returns in the clean energy sector or if renewable assets are governed primarily by systematic market risk.

Quantitative Asset Pricing & Econometric Modeling — Vestas Wind Systems vs. Brent Crude

### Key Features

* **Automated Financial Pipeline:** Fully automated data extraction via R (`quantmod`) querying adjusted prices, utilizing monthly log-returns to eliminate time-zone mismatch and trading calendar discrepancies across CET and USD markets[cite: 3].
* **Multi-Factor Econometric Specification:** Evaluates stock sensitivity by regressing asset performance against a market benchmark (OMXC25), commodity variations (Brent Oil), and sectoral trends (ICLN Clean Energy ETF)[cite: 3].
* **Fat-Tail Volatility & Outlier Management:** Detects and handles severe non-normality in oil returns (excess kurtosis $\approx 19.8$, skewness $\approx -2.88$) generated by exogenous market shocks[cite: 3].
* **HAC Robust Inference:** Implements Newey-West robust standard errors to ensure valid inference despite heavy-tailed non-normal residual distributions[cite: 3].
* **CLRM Diagnostic Suite:** Executes formal Breusch-Pagan, Breusch-Godfrey, and correlation matrix checks to verify classical linear regression assumptions[cite: 3].
* **Empirical Hypothesis Testing:** Formally isolates and tests the cross-commodity substitution coefficient ($\beta_2$), demonstrating that clean energy valuations decouple from short-term fossil fuel swings[cite: 3].

### 📊 Econometric Framework & Specification

The model isolates the specific impact of crude oil prices while controlling for broader macroeconomic market returns and clean energy sector sentiment[cite: 3]:

### 💾 Empirical Architecture & Model Specification

The econometric analysis is implemented in **R** and enforces strict statistical validity through formal diagnostics and robust standard errors[cite: 3].

#### 1. Core Variables & Time Series Data
* **Dependent Variable:** $r_t^{\text{Vestas}}$ (`VWS.CO`) — Monthly log-returns of Vestas Wind Systems ($N = 107$, Dec 2016 – 2025)[cite: 3].
* **Market Benchmark:** $r_t^{\text{Market}}$ (`^OMXC25`) — Monthly log-returns of the Danish OMX Copenhagen 25 Index (Systematic market risk proxy)[cite: 3].
* **Commodity Driver:** $r_t^{\text{Oil}}$ (`BZ=F`) — Monthly log-returns of Brent Crude Oil futures (Fossil fuel substitution proxy)[cite: 3].
* **Sector Benchmark:** $r_t^{\text{Clean}}$ (`ICLN`) — Monthly log-returns of iShares Global Clean Energy ETF (Sectoral trend proxy)[cite: 3].

#### 2. Estimation Engine & Methodological Controls
* **Estimation Technique:** Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) minimizing the Residual Sum of Squares (RSS)[cite: 3].
* **Standard Error Correction:** Newey-West Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent (HAC) estimators[cite: 3].
* **Multicollinearity Constraint:** Pairwise correlations across independent variables strictly verified below $0.60$ ($\max r_{x_i, x_j} = 0.558$ between Market and Clean Energy)[cite: 3].

#### 3. Diagnostic Verifications & Residual Analysis
* **Heteroscedasticity:** Breusch-Pagan Test ($BP = 3.5993$, $df = 3$, $p\text{-value} = 0.3081$) $\rightarrow$ Failure to reject homoscedasticity[cite: 3].
* **Autocorrelation:** Breusch-Godfrey Test ($LM = 0.0077$, $df = 1$, $p\text{-value} = 0.9300$) $\rightarrow$ No serial correlation in error terms[cite: 3].
* **Distribution Properties:** Residual analysis reveals fat-tailed leptokurtic distribution inherited from oil price volatility spikes[cite: 3].
